Good to know

Questions, answered.

Short answers about what Volume Guard does, where it works and what it keeps private. Missing something? Ask on GitHub.

Using it

What does Volume Guard actually do?

It keeps a video at the level you chose. When a song or an action scene gets loud, it turns the sound down quickly. When people talk quietly, it lifts the sound gently, up to a limit. It never changes the video itself, only how loud it plays.

How do I set my level?

Open the popup and turn the dial, use the slider, or pick a preset: Night, Normal or Cinema. Normal sits at 68 percent. See Presets and settings.

Will it make quiet scenes much louder?

Only up to a limit you control: 6 dB on Normal, 9 dB on Night, never more than 12 dB. Boosting more would also lift background hiss, so the limit is deliberate. Silence is never boosted.

A sudden bang still comes through for a moment. Why?

Volume Guard cannot hear ahead without delaying the sound, which would put it out of sync with the picture. The first fraction of a second of a sudden loud cut passes through, and a limiter keeps that peak in check.

How do I turn it off quickly?

Press Alt+Shift+V, use the switch at the top of the popup, or choose Pause for 15 min. You can change the shortcut in your browser's extension shortcut settings.

Sites and limits

Which sites does it work on?

Most sites that play video in the browser, including players inside iframes. To have it start by itself on a site, turn on Always on for this site in the popup; your browser asks for permission for that site only.

What about Netflix and other protected streams?

Copy-protected video cannot be adjusted directly by any extension. Volume Guard notices this, leaves the sound exactly as it was and tells you why. An experimental Compatibility mode can level the whole tab's audio instead. It asks for extra permission only when you turn it on, may add a small delay, and has not yet been verified on real protected sites.

Which browsers are supported?

Chrome and Microsoft Edge, version 114 or newer. Firefox and Safari are not supported yet.

Does it work with the player's own volume control?

Yes. The correction is applied on top of the player's volume, so the player's slider and mute keep working as usual.

Privacy

Does it see what I watch?

No. It does not read page content, video titles or your browsing history. It only uses a site's name (for example www.youtube.com) to apply your per-site choice, and that stays on your device.

Is my audio recorded or sent anywhere?

No. Sound is measured in memory to decide how loud to play it and then discarded. The extension makes no network requests and has no analytics, telemetry or advertising.

Why does it ask for permissions?

At install it needs storage for your settings and access to the tab you are looking at, only after you open the popup. Access to a whole site, and the two permissions for Compatibility mode, are requested only when you turn those features on. Details are in Privacy and permissions.

The project

Is it free?

Yes. It has no ads, accounts or paid tiers, and the source code is public.

Where can I get it?

It is not in the Chrome Web Store yet. You can build it from the source code today: Getting started walks you through it in about five minutes.

Who makes it?

Volume Guard is made by Nur Hasan Masum, a lifelong movie fan who got tired of reaching for the volume button.

How can I report a problem or suggest an idea?

Open an issue on GitHub. The support page lists what to include so it can be fixed quickly.
